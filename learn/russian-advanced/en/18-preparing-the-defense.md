# Lesson 18. Preparing the Defense: The Talk and the Slides

> **Advanced Russian for Researchers** · Level: C1 · **You will learn:** the formal opening and closing of a defense talk · signposting phrases that guide listeners from slide to slide · how to turn written dissertation sentences into spoken Russian and into slide text

## Text

Before the defense, the dissertation is usually discussed at the department (this preliminary discussion is often called the **предзащи́та**), and the candidate rehearses the talk many times. Below is part of Minho's hypothetical defense talk, with the slide numbers, followed by a rehearsal at the department where Ivan Petrovich and Anna comment on it.

## Grammar

### 1. Opening and closing a defense talk

A defense is a formal public event, and the talk begins and ends with fixed formulas.

**Address.** The speaker addresses the audience in order of rank: *Уважаемый председатель, уважаемые члены диссертационного совета, уважаемые оппоненты, коллеги!* The adjective is always **уважа́емый** ("respected"); *дорогие* ("dear") is far too personal for this situation. After *члены* the genitive follows: *члены диссертационного совета*. Customs differ slightly between councils (some speakers also address the chair by name and patronymic), so listen to how others do it at earlier defenses.

**Presenting the topic.** The standard formula is impersonal: *Вашему вниманию предлагается диссертационная работа на тему «…»* (literally "to your attention is offered a dissertation on the topic…"). The verb agrees with *работа* (singular). A slightly less formal alternative is *Позвольте представить…* ("allow me to present…").

**Thanks.** *Выразить благодарность* takes the dative of the person: *своему научному руководителю, профессору Ивану Петровичу Смирнову*. Every word in the chain is in the dative. In speech, a person is named first name + patronymic + surname; the surname-first order belongs to written lists.

**Ending.** The talk ends with two short sentences: *Доклад окончен. Благодарю за внимание.* (or *Спасибо за внимание.*). Then you wait for the chair to open the questions.

- Уважаемый председатель, уважаемые члены диссертационного совета! — *Mr/Madam Chair, distinguished members of the dissertation council!*
- Вашему вниманию предлагается диссертационная работа на тему «…». — *I present to you the dissertation on the topic "…".*
- Позвольте представить основные результаты работы. — *Allow me to present the main results of the work.*
- Хочу выразить благодарность своему научному руководителю. — *I would like to thank my supervisor.*
- Доклад окончен. Благодарю за внимание. — *That concludes my talk. Thank you for your attention.*

### 2. Signposting: guiding the listener

A listener cannot go back a page, so a spoken talk needs much more signposting than a written text. Each transition announces what comes next. Pay attention to the case each verb takes.

| Function | Russian formula |
|---|---|
| Beginning | Прежде всего несколько слов о (чём?)… |
| Moving on | Перейду к (чему?)…; Перейдём к (чему?)… |
| Looking at something in detail | Теперь остановлюсь на (чём?)… |
| Turning to a new topic | Обратимся к (чему?)… |
| Pointing to the slide | На слайде представлен(а, о, ы)…; Схема показана на слайде. |
| Referring to a table or graph | Как видно из (чего?) таблицы / графика… |
| Listing | Во-первых…, во-вторых…, в-третьих… |
| Summing up | Подводя итог…; Таким образом… |
| Closing | В заключение хочу… |

The case government matters: *перейти́ к* + dative (*к первому результату*), *останови́ться на* + prepositional (*на предложенном методе*), *обрати́ться к* + dative (*к результатам экспериментов*), *как ви́дно из* + genitive (*из таблицы*). With *представлен*, check agreement with the noun that follows: *представлена схема*, *представлены характеристики*.

Note the forms *перейду́* and *остановлю́сь*: the speaker uses the 1st person singular, perfective future, to announce what he is about to do. *Перейдём* and *обратимся* (1st person plural) invite the audience to come along. Both are normal in a talk, while the dissertation itself avoids *я*.

### 3. From written text to talk, from talk to slide

**Written → spoken.** The general characteristics are written for reading: long sentences, participial phrases, chains of nouns. Read aloud, they are hard to follow. For the talk, keep the content but change the form:
- split one long sentence into two or three short ones;
- replace participial phrases with simple clauses or separate sentences;
- replace verbal nouns with verbs (*разработка методов* → *нужно разработать методы*);
- use a rhetorical question to introduce a point (*Почему эта тема актуальна?*);
- use simple connectors: *поэтому, при этом, значит, благодаря этому*.

- Почему эта тема актуальна? — *Why is this topic relevant?*
- Языковые модели обучаются в основном на английских данных. — *Language models are trained mainly on English data.*
- Поэтому с русскими научными текстами они работают хуже. — *That is why they work worse with Russian scientific texts.*
- Значит, нужны методы адаптации. — *So we need adaptation methods.*

There is one important exception: the **aim, the novelty and the statements for the defense** are usually given in the talk exactly as in the dissertation and the avtoreferat, because the council compares them. Simplify the explanations around them, not the official wording.

**Spoken → slide.** A slide is not a page of the dissertation. Slide headings and bullet points are **noun phrases**, not sentences: no verbs, no *мы*, no full stops at the end of the items. A verbal noun usually replaces the verb (*мы учитываем частоту* → *учёт частоты*; *мы сравнили* → *сравнение*). Numbers, tables and diagrams carry the details; you say the rest.

- Цель и задачи исследования — *Aim and tasks of the research*
- Корпус научных текстов — *The corpus of scientific texts*
- Предложенный метод — *The proposed method*
- Результаты экспериментов — *Experimental results*
- Основные результаты — *Main results*
- Учёт частоты научных терминов — *Taking the frequency of scientific terms into account*

## Culture

**What happens at a defense, in general terms.** A typical Russian defense is a formal public meeting of the dissertation council. In general, it includes the candidate's talk, questions from the council members and the audience, the reading or presentation of the reviews (from the supervisor, the leading organization, other specialists and the official opponents), the candidate's answers to the remarks, a discussion, and finally the council's vote. The talk has a strict time limit (**регла́мент**) set by the council, and the chair will stop a speaker who runs over. The exact order and rules depend on the council, so attend one or two defenses in your department before your own: it is the best way to hear the formulas from this lesson in real use. We will practise answering the opponents' and the council's questions in the next lesson.

Checklist:
- The address uses *уважаемый/уважаемые* and the genitive after *члены*: *члены диссертационного совета*.
- The topic is introduced with *Вашему вниманию предлагается диссертационная работа на тему…* and the title is in quotation marks «…».
- The transition to relevance is signposted: *Прежде всего несколько слов о…*
- The sentences are short, with simple connectors (*но, а, поэтому*), and could be said in one breath.
- The paragraph ends with the gap that the work fills.

## Review

- You can open a defense talk with the correct address and present your topic with *Вашему вниманию предлагается…*
- You can guide listeners with *перейду к…, остановлюсь на…, обратимся к…, как видно из…*, using the right cases.
- You can thank your supervisor with a correct dative chain and end with *Доклад окончен. Благодарю за внимание.*
- You can turn written dissertation sentences into short spoken ones while keeping the official wording of the aim and novelty.
- You can write slide headings and bullet points as noun phrases.
