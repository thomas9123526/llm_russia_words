# Lesson 3. Pronunciation: Stress, Vowel Reduction, Hard and Soft Consonants, Devoicing

> **Russian for Beginners** · Level: A1 · **You will learn:** why stress matters and how unstressed о, е, я change · hard and soft consonants · why хлеб sounds like [khlep], and how to buy food in a canteen

You can now read every letter. But if you read Russian *letter by letter*, Russians will understand you only with effort, because spoken Russian follows four rules that spelling does not show:

1. **Stress**: one syllable in each word is strong; the others are weak.
2. **Vowel reduction**: vowels in weak (unstressed) syllables change — **о** often sounds like [a].
3. **Hard and soft consonants**: most consonants exist in two versions.
4. **Devoicing**: at the end of a word, [b], [d], [g]… turn into [p], [t], [k]….

These rules are regular. Once you know them, you can predict the pronunciation of almost any word from its spelling plus the stress mark. In this lesson we write the real pronunciation in square brackets in a simple Latin form, for example **молоко́** [malakó]. A soft consonant has an apostrophe: [n'et].

Do not try to be perfect today. Read the examples aloud many times; your ear will slowly get used to the rhythm.

## Dialogue

*Minho buys food in the university canteen.*

**Минхо́:** Здра́вствуйте! Молоко́, пожа́луйста.  
Zdrávstvuyte! Molokó, pozháluysta.  
*Hello! Milk, please.*

**Продавщи́ца:** Вот молоко́. А хлеб?  
Vot molokó. A khleb?  
*Here's the milk. And bread?*

**Минхо́:** Да, хлеб и сыр, пожа́луйста.  
Da, khleb i syr, pozháluysta.  
*Yes, bread and cheese, please.*

**Продавщи́ца:** Вот хлеб и сыр. Всё?  
Vot khleb i syr. Vsyo?  
*Here are the bread and cheese. Is that all?*

**Минхо́:** И сок, пожа́луйста.  
I sok, pozháluysta.  
*And juice, please.*

**Продавщи́ца:** Вот сок.  
Vot sok.  
*Here's the juice.*

**Минхо́:** Спаси́бо!  
Spasíbo!  
*Thank you!*

**Продавщи́ца:** Пожа́луйста. До свида́ния!  
Pozháluysta. Do svidániya!  
*You're welcome. Goodbye!*

**Минхо́:** До свида́ния!  
Do svidániya!  
*Goodbye!*

*Pronunciation: Здра́вствуйте* [zdrástvuyte] *— the first* в *is silent;* пожа́луйста [pazhálsta]*;* молоко́ [malakó]*;* хлеб [khlep]*.*

## Grammar

### 1. Stress (ударе́ние)

In every Russian word of two or more syllables, **one** syllable is stressed. The stressed vowel is louder, longer and clearer than the others. Russian stress is **free**: it can fall on any syllable, and there is no simple rule to find it.

| First syllable | Middle syllable | Last syllable |
|---|---|---|
| ма́ма | спаси́бо | молоко́ |
| го́род | пожа́луйста | вода́ |
| у́тро | компью́тер | рестора́н |

Stress can change the meaning of a word:

- за́мок [zámok] — *castle*
- замо́к [zamók] — *lock*
- му́ка [múka] — *torment*
- мука́ [muká] — *flour*

So always learn a new word **together with its stress**. Say it aloud with the stress three times. In ordinary Russian texts there are no stress marks, so your memory is the only place where the stress is kept.

### 2. Vowel reduction: unstressed о, е, я

This is the most important rule of this lesson. Only the stressed vowel keeps its full sound. In unstressed syllables:

| Letter | Stressed | Unstressed | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| о | [o] | [a] (in fast speech often a short [ə]) | молоко́ [malakó], вода́ [vadá], хорошо́ [kharashó] |
| а | [a] | [a] / short [ə] | ма́ма [mámə], Москва́ [maskvá] |
| е | [ye], [e] | close to [i] | сестра́ [sistrá], сего́дня [sivódnya] |
| я | [ya] | close to [yi] / [i] | язы́к [yizýk] |
| у, ы, и | no change | no change | у́тро, ры́ба, кино́ |

Look at **молоко́** (*milk*): three letters **о**, but only the last one is stressed. So the word sounds [malakó]. In the same way **хорошо́** sounds [kharashó], and **Москва́** sounds [maskvá]. This "a-sound" pronunciation of **о** is called **а́канье**, and it is the standard Moscow pronunciation.

Unstressed **е** and **я** both move towards [i]: **сестра́** [sistrá], **язы́к** [yizýk]. This is called **и́канье**.

**Reading drill 1.** Read the spelling, then the real pronunciation.

- молоко́ = [malakó] — *milk*
- вода́ = [vadá] — *water*
- окно́ = [aknó] — *window*
- хорошо́ = [kharashó] — *good, well*
- Москва́ = [maskvá] — *Moscow*
- го́род = [górat] — *city*
- сестра́ = [sistrá] — *sister*
- сего́дня = [sivódnya] — *today*
- язы́к = [yizýk] — *language*
- моде́ль = [mad'él'] — *model*

(In **го́род** [górat] you can see two rules at once: the unstressed **о** becomes [a], and the final **д** becomes [t] — see section 4.)

### 3. Hard and soft consonants

Most Russian consonants come in two versions: **hard** and **soft**. To make a soft consonant, raise the middle of your tongue towards the roof of your mouth while you say it, as if you were about to say [y]. A soft consonant is not "consonant + y + vowel"; it is one sound with a different colour.

A consonant is **soft** when it stands before **е, ё, и, ю, я** or before **ь**. Otherwise it is usually **hard**.

| Hard | Soft | |
|---|---|---|
| брат (*brother*) | брать (*to take*) | soft т before ь |
| лук (*onion*) | люк (*hatch*) | soft л before ю |
| у́гол (*corner*) | у́голь (*coal*) | soft л before ь |
| нос (*nose*) | нет (*no*) | soft н before е |
| мы (*we*) | мир (*world*) | soft м before и |

This is why the vowel letters come in pairs (Lesson 2): **а, у, о, э, ы** say "the consonant before me is hard"; **я, ю, ё, е, и** say "the consonant before me is soft".

Three consonants are **always hard**: **ж, ш, ц**. Even before **и** they stay hard, and the **и** then sounds like [ɨ]: **маши́на** [mashýna] (*car*), **жить** [zhyt'] (*to live*). Three consonants are **always soft**: **ч, щ, й**.

**Reading drill 2.** Read the pairs and feel the difference.

- брат — брать [brat — brat'] — *brother — to take*
- лук — люк [luk — lyuk] — *onion — hatch*
- у́гол — у́голь [úgol — úgol'] — *corner — coal*
- мат — мать [mat — mat'] — *checkmate — mother*
- нос — нёс [nos — nyos] — *nose — carried*
- мы — ми́ло [my — mílo] — *we — (it's) sweet*

### 4. Devoicing and voicing

Russian consonants come in voiced/voiceless pairs:

| Voiced | б | в | г | д | ж | з |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Voiceless** | **п** | **ф** | **к** | **т** | **ш** | **с** |

**Rule A — end of the word.** A voiced consonant at the end of a word becomes voiceless:

- хлеб = [khlep] — *bread*
- сад = [sat] — *garden*
- друг = [druk] — *friend*
- нож = [nosh] — *knife*
- глаз = [glas] — *eye*
- Смирно́в = [smirnóf] — *Smirnov (surname)*

**Rule B — inside the word.** When two consonants meet, the second one decides: before a voiceless consonant a voiced one becomes voiceless, and before a voiced one (except в, л, м, н, р, й) a voiceless one becomes voiced.

- авто́бус = [aftóbus] — *bus*
- ло́жка = [lóshka] — *spoon*
- вокза́л = [vagzál] — *railway station*

### 5. Words that are not read as written

A few very common words have special pronunciations. Learn them as they are:

| Word | Pronunciation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| что | [shto] | *what* |
| его́ | [yivó] | *him, his* (г = [v]) |
| сего́дня | [sivódnya] | *today* (г = [v]) |
| здра́вствуйте | [zdrástvuyte] | *hello* (first в silent) |
| пожа́луйста | [pazhálsta] | *please* (у and й are not heard) |
| спаси́бо | [spasíba] | *thank you* |

## Study tip

**Read in three steps.** For every new word: (1) find the stress mark; (2) turn unstressed **о** into [a] and unstressed **е/я** into [i]; (3) check the last consonant for devoicing. Try it with **Смирно́в**: stress on **о́**; no other **о**; final **в** → [f]: [smirnóf]. After a few weeks you will do this automatically. Record yourself on your phone and compare with a native speaker — dictionaries online often have audio.

## Review

- You know that every word has one stressed syllable, and that stress can change meaning (**за́мок / замо́к**).
- You pronounce unstressed **о** as [a] (**молоко́** [malakó]) and unstressed **е, я** close to [i] (**сестра́** [sistrá]).
- You can hear and make the difference between hard and soft consonants (**брат / брать**).
- You devoice final consonants: **хлеб** [khlep], **Смирно́в** [smirnóf].
- You can buy food: **Молоко́, пожа́луйста. — Спаси́бо! — До свида́ния!**
