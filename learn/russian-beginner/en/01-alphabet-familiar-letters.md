# Lesson 1. The Alphabet I: Familiar Letters and False Friends

> **Russian for Beginners** · Level: A1 · **You will learn:** five letters that look and sound like English · seven "false friends" that look English but sound different · to read your first Russian words and a short dialogue

Russian is written in the **Cyrillic alphabet** (кири́ллица). It has 33 letters. That sounds like a lot, but there is good news: many Cyrillic letters look like Latin letters, and Russian spelling is much closer to pronunciation than English spelling. Once you know a letter, you can usually read it the same way in every word.

In this lesson you learn **12 letters**, in two groups:

1. **Five "true friends"**: А, К, М, О, Т. They look like English letters and sound almost the same.
2. **Seven "false friends"**: В, Н, Р, С, У, Х, Е. They look like English letters, but they stand for *different* sounds. These are the dangerous ones, because your eyes will want to read them "in English".

With only these 12 letters you can already read real Russian words: **Москва́**, **метро́**, **рестора́н**, **теа́тр**, **текст**. By the end of the lesson you will read a short dialogue.

**How to use this book.** Every Russian word of two or more syllables has a small mark over one vowel, for example **ма́ма**. This is the **stress mark**: say that vowel louder and longer. Ordinary Russian books and websites do not show it — it is a learning aid. Under every line of a dialogue you will find a transliteration in Latin letters (for example *Vot Moskvá!*). Use it only to check yourself: try to read the Cyrillic first.

## Dialogue

*Anna shows Minho the city on his first day in Moscow.*

**А́нна:** Вот Москва́!  
Vot Moskvá!  
*Here is Moscow!*

**Минхо́:** Ура́! А там метро́?  
Urá! A tam metró?  
*Hooray! And is the metro over there?*

**А́нна:** Нет, там теа́тр.  
Net, tam teátr.  
*No, there's a theatre over there.*

**Минхо́:** А тут?  
A tut?  
*And here?*

**А́нна:** Тут метро́.  
Tut metró.  
*The metro is here.*

**Минхо́:** А там рестора́н?  
A tam restorán?  
*And is that a restaurant over there?*

**А́нна:** Нет, там мост.  
Net, tam most.  
*No, that's a bridge over there.*

**Минхо́:** О, а вот кот!  
O, a vot kot!  
*Oh, and here's a cat!*

**А́нна:** Кот? Тут?  
Kot? Tut?  
*A cat? Here?*

**Минхо́:** Вот он!  
Vot on!  
*There he is!*

*Note: the names* А́нна *and* Минхо́ *are written in Cyrillic. The letter* и *in* Минхо́ *is from Lesson 2; it sounds like* [i] *in "machine".*

## Grammar

In the first lessons, "grammar" means the alphabet itself. Read each table aloud, then do the reading drill below it.

### 1. Five true friends: А, К, М, О, Т

These letters look like English letters and sound (almost) like them. Every Cyrillic letter has a capital and a small form; for these five the small form is simply a smaller copy of the capital. Notice that small **к**, **м**, **т** look like little capitals, not like English *k*, *m*, *t*.

| Letter | Sound | Like English… | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| А а | [a] | *a* in *father* | ма́ма |
| К к | [k] | *k* in *skate* (no puff of air) | кот |
| М м | [m] | *m* in *map* | мост |
| О о | [o] | *o* in *more*, when stressed | он |
| Т т | [t] | *t* in *stop* (no puff of air) | тут |

Two small points. First, Russian **к** and **т** are pronounced without the little puff of air that English has at the start of *kit* or *top*. Say *skate* and *stop*: the *k* and *t* there are exactly Russian **к** and **т**. Second, **о** sounds like a clear [o] only when it is stressed. In Lesson 3 you will see that an unstressed **о** changes; for now, read it as [o].

**Reading drill 1.** Read each word aloud, then check the transliteration.

- там [tam] — *there*
- так [tak] — *so, like this*
- как [kak] — *how*
- кот [kot] — *cat*
- том [tom] — *volume (of a book)*
- ма́ма [máma] — *mum*
- а́том [átom] — *atom*
- ата́ка [atáka] — *attack*
- ко́мната [kómnata] — *room*

### 2. Seven false friends: В, Н, Р, С, У, Х, Е

These letters are traps. They look like English letters, but they stand for other sounds. Learn them as completely new letters.

| Letter | Looks like… | Real sound | How to say it | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| В в | B | [v] | *v* in *very* | вот |
| Н н | H | [n] | *n* in *no* | нет |
| Р р | P | [r] | rolled *r*, tongue tip taps behind the teeth | рот |
| С с | C | [s] | always *s* in *sun*, never *k* | сок |
| У у | Y | [u] | *oo* in *boot*, lips round | тут |
| Х х | X | [kh] | *ch* in Scottish *loch* | са́хар |
| Е е | E | [ye] | *ye* in *yes* | нет |

Some notes:

- **В** is a [v], never a [b]. The Russian letter for [b] is a different one (Lesson 2).
- **Н** is the Russian [n]. The small form **н** looks like a small capital H.
- **Р** is a rolled or tapped *r*, like the *r* in Spanish or Italian. The small **р** looks like English *p*, but it is never [p].
- **С** is always [s]. Russian has no "hard c" — the [k] sound is always **к**.
- **У** is [u]. The small **у** looks like English *y*; do not read it as [y]!
- **Х** is a rough [kh] made at the back of the mouth, like clearing your throat gently. It is not [ks].
- **Е** is read [ye] at the start of a word and after a vowel. After a consonant it sounds like [e] and also makes that consonant "soft": **нет** sounds like [n'et]. You will learn about soft consonants in Lesson 3.

**Reading drill 2.** Read slowly and do not let the "English" readings in.

- вот [vot] — *here is*
- нет [net] — *no*
- нос [nos] — *nose*
- рот [rot] — *mouth*
- сок [sok] — *juice*
- тут [tut] — *here*
- ура́ [urá] — *hooray*
- рука́ [ruká] — *hand, arm*
- му́ха [múkha] — *fly (insect)*
- са́хар [sákhar] — *sugar*
- сестра́ [sestrá] — *sister*
- у́тро [útro] — *morning*
- ме́сто [mésto] — *place, seat*

### 3. "Fake English" words and international words

Because of the false friends, some Russian words written in capitals look like English words that do not exist. Shop signs are often in capitals, so it is worth practising. Read these the Russian way:

- НЕТ [NET] — *no (not "het")*
- ВОТ [VOT] — *here is (not "bot")*
- РОТ [ROT] — *mouth (not "pot")*
- СА́ХАР [SÁKHAR] — *sugar (not "caxap")*
- РЕСТОРА́Н [RESTORÁN] — *restaurant (not "pectopah")*
- МОСКВА́ [MOSKVÁ] — *Moscow*

Many Russian words are international, so you already know their meaning once you can read them. Here are some built only from this lesson's letters — two of them come from your own field:

- метро́ [metró] — *metro*
- теа́тр [teátr] — *theatre*
- рестора́н [restorán] — *restaurant*
- а́втор [ávtor] — *author*
- ко́смос [kósmos] — *outer space*
- текст [tekst] — *text*
- ве́ктор [véktor] — *vector*

### 4. Three small words for the dialogue

You do not need grammar yet, but three little words make the dialogue work:

- **Вот** points at something: *here is, there is, look*. **Вот кот!** — *Here's a cat!*
- **Тут** means *here*, **там** means *there*. Russian needs no verb "is" in such sentences: **Тут метро́.** — *The metro is here.* (literally "here metro").
- **А** joins two parts with a small contrast or a change of topic: **А тут?** — *And (what about) here?*

## Study tip

**Handwriting and italics.** In handwriting and in italic print some letters change shape. The most surprising one is small **т**: in handwriting it looks like English *m*. The handwritten capital **В** looks like a "B" with a loop, and handwritten **у** has a long tail like *y*. For now, read printed letters; just remember that *т* in italics may look like *m*. A good habit: copy each new word by hand three times, saying it aloud each time.

- You can read 12 Cyrillic letters: **А К М О Т** (true friends) and **В Н Р С У Х Е** (false friends).
- You know that **В** = [v], **Н** = [n], **Р** = [r], **С** = [s], **У** = [u], **Х** = [kh], **Е** = [ye].
