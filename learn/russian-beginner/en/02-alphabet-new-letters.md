# Lesson 2. The Alphabet II: New Letters

> **Russian for Beginners** · Level: A1 · **You will learn:** the remaining 21 letters of the alphabet · the vowel pairs а/я, у/ю, о/ё, э/е, ы/и and the signs ь and ъ · to read international words from your field: то́кен, моде́ль, компью́тер

In Lesson 1 you learnt 12 letters. Today you meet the other **21**, and after this lesson you can read *any* Russian word. That is a big step, so do not hurry: work through the tables one group at a time, read every drill aloud, and come back to this lesson whenever a letter feels uncertain.

The new letters fall into four groups:

1. **Consonants with familiar sounds** but new shapes: Б, Г, Д, З, Л, П, Ф.
2. **Hissing and hushing sounds** and the short **й**: Ж, Ц, Ч, Ш, Щ, Й.
3. **Vowels**: И, Ы, Э and the "y-vowels" Ю, Я, Ё.
4. **Two signs with no sound of their own**: Ь and Ъ.

Here is the whole alphabet in its official order. You do not need to memorise the order now, but you will need it later to use a dictionary.

**А Б В Г Д Е Ё Ж З И Й К Л М Н О П Р С Т У Ф Х Ц Ч Ш Щ Ъ Ы Ь Э Ю Я**

## Dialogue

*Anna looks at Minho's laptop in the university library.*

**А́нна:** Э́то компью́тер?  
Éto komp'yúter?  
*Is this a computer?*

**Минхо́:** Нет, э́то ноутбу́к.  
Net, éto noutbúk.  
*No, it's a laptop.*

**А́нна:** А что э́то?  
A chto éto?  
*And what's this?*

**Минхо́:** Э́то моде́ль.  
Éto modél'.  
*It's a model.*

**А́нна:** Нейросе́ть?  
Neyrosét'?  
*A neural network?*

**Минхо́:** Да. Э́то чат-бо́т.  
Da. Éto chat-bót.  
*Yes. It's a chatbot.*

**А́нна:** Чат-бо́т? Интере́сно!  
Chat-bót? Interésno!  
*A chatbot? Interesting!*

**Минхо́:** Вот, смотри́!  
Vot, smotrí!  
*Here, look!*

**А́нна:** Ой, он по-ру́сски?  
Oy, on po-rússki?  
*Oh, does it speak Russian?*

**Минхо́:** Да, и по-коре́йски.  
Da, i po-koréyski.  
*Yes, and Korean too.*

*Note: in* что *the letters* чт *are pronounced* [sht]*: say* [shto]*. The transliteration shows the letters (*chto*), not the sound.*

## Grammar

### 1. Consonants with familiar sounds: Б, Г, Д, З, Л, П, Ф

These letters have new shapes but sounds you already know from English.

| Letter | Sound | Like English… | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Б б | [b] | *b* in *book* | банк |
| Г г | [g] | *g* in *go* (always hard, never like *gem*) | го́род |
| Д д | [d] | *d* in *do* | дом |
| З з | [z] | *z* in *zoo* | зал |
| Л л | [l] | "dark" *l* in *full* | ла́мпа |
| П п | [p] | *p* in *spin* (no puff of air) | парк |
| Ф ф | [f] | *f* in *fun* | фо́то |

Watch the shapes: **Б** (b) and **В** (v) are different letters; **П** looks like the Greek letter *pi*; **Д** has two little "feet"; **З** looks like the number 3. **Г** is always [g], even before **е** and **и**. Russian **л** is darker than the English *l* in *leaf*: the tip of the tongue touches the upper teeth and the back of the tongue rises, as in English *full* or *milk*.

### 2. Hissing and hushing sounds: Ж, Ц, Ч, Ш, Щ, and short Й

These are the letters that look most "foreign". Take them one by one.

| Letter | Sound | How to say it | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ж ж | [zh] | *s* in *pleasure*, but with the tongue further back | журна́л |
| Ц ц | [ts] | *ts* in *cats* | центр |
| Ч ч | [ch] | *ch* in *cheap*, soft | чай |
| Ш ш | [sh] | *sh* in *shop*, but harder, tongue further back | шко́ла |
| Щ щ | [shch] | a long, soft *sh*, as in *fresh sheep* said quickly | борщ |
| Й й | [y] | short *y* in *boy*, *toy* | май |

**Ж** and **Ш** are a pair: **ж** is voiced, **ш** is voiceless. Both are "hard" — the tongue is pulled back and the middle of the tongue is low. **Щ** is the opposite: soft, long, with the tongue high. Many Russians simply pronounce it as a long soft [sh':]. **Й** is not a full vowel; it is a short [y] glide that usually comes after a vowel: **май** [may], **чай** [chay].

### 3. Vowels: И, Ы, Э and the y-vowels Ю, Я, Ё

Russian has ten vowel letters but only five or six basic vowel sounds. The letters come in **pairs**: one letter for after a "hard" consonant, one for after a "soft" consonant (or for the sound [y] + vowel at the start of a word).

| "Hard" vowel | Sound | "Soft" vowel | Sound at the start of a word / after a vowel |
|---|---|---|---|
| а | [a] | я | [ya] |
| у | [u] | ю | [yu] |
| о | [o] | ё | [yo] |
| э | [e] | е | [ye] |
| ы | [ɨ] | и | [i] |

- **И и** = [i] as in *machine*: **мир** (*world, peace*), **и** (*and*).
- **Ы ы** = a sound English does not have. Put your tongue as for [u] in *book*, but keep your lips flat and spread, as if smiling. Say **ты** (*you*), **мы** (*we*), **сыр** (*cheese*). It is not [i]: **мир** and **мы** have different vowels.
- **Э э** = [e] as in *met*: **э́то** (*this is*). It is rare except at the start of words.
- **Я я**, **Ю ю**, **Ё ё** = [ya], [yu], [yo] at the start of a word or after a vowel: **я** (*I*), **юг** (*south*), **ёлка** (*fir tree*). After a consonant they make the consonant soft (Lesson 3).
- **Ё** is always stressed, so it never needs a stress mark. In ordinary texts Russians often write **е** instead of **ё**; in this book we always write **ё**.

### 4. The signs Ь and Ъ

These two letters have **no sound of their own**.

- **Ь ь** — the **soft sign**. It makes the consonant before it soft, as if a tiny [y] were added without a vowel: **мать** [mat'] (*mother*), **день** [den'] (*day*), **соль** [sol'] (*salt*). It is very common, also in your field: **моде́ль**, **нейросе́ть**, **слова́рь** (*dictionary*). In transliteration it is written with an apostrophe: *model'*.
- **Ъ ъ** — the **hard sign**. It is rare. It stands between a consonant and **е, ё, ю, я** and shows that the consonant stays hard and a separate [y] is heard before the vowel: in **объе́кт** (*object*) you say **б**, then [y] + **е́** — *ob-yékt*. The same happens in **подъе́зд** (*entrance of a building*). In transliteration it is written ʺ: *obʺékt*.

## Culture

**Your name in Cyrillic.** Korean names are written in Cyrillic by sound. The common surnames 김, 이, 박 become **Ким**, **Ли**, **Пак**; the city of Seoul is **Сеу́л**, and Korea is **Коре́я**. Our hero 민호 is **Минхо́**: the Korean ㅎ is written with **х**. Try to write your own name in Cyrillic — you will need it for every form at the university.
